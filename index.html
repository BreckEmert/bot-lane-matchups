How these numbers are made

Raw "win rate vs X" tables mostly measure how strong each champion is overall, mixed with who they tend to lane with and who tends to play them. This sheet fits one Bayesian logistic regression on every game, so each effect is estimated holding the others fixed, then shrinks noisy cells toward zero by an amount the data itself chooses.

Checked against simulated games with known, planted effects: the 90% intervals contained the true counter 89% of the time and the true edge 90% of the time. The same test shows why lane measures matter. A single support-vs-ADC counter is small next to win/loss noise, while CS@10 and gold@14 carry far more signal per game.

How much each part moves a real game

How far each part typically pushes a game's win probability (1 SD across games), implied by the fitted effect sizes, so the size of a counter can be read next to everything else that decides a game. Player situation uses the fitted controls directly and is noisy until the crawl finishes.

A counter is not a strong champion

A support that wins 53% into everyone also wins 53% into Ezreal; that is not a counter. The "Counter only" column is double-centered: S's edge vs X beyond S's average matchup and beyond X's average matchup, weighted by pick rates.

The enemy support comes with the ADC

Draven players lane with engage supports far more often, so "Lulu vs Draven" quietly measures Lulu vs Leona too. Support-vs-support and ADC-vs-ADC effects are separate terms. "Edge" averages over who X actually lanes with; the 2v2 view lets you name the support.

Your own ADC matters

Enchanters are picked with hypercarries, so their raw numbers inherit the carry's strength. Support + own-ADC synergy is its own term, averaged over a typical partner unless you set one in the 2v2 view.

The other eight champions

Some bot laners get picked into comps that are strong for other reasons. All ten champions get role-specific strength terms, plus blue-side advantage.

Autofill and secondary roles

Support is the most-filled role, and filled players lose more and pick simpler champions. Riot's own role-assignment field (primary / secondary / autofill / fill) is a control for every player, along with role swaps.

One-tricks vs first-timers

Niche picks are played by mains, popular ones by everyone. Mastery is only readable after the game, though, and players who win on a new champion keep playing it, so a fine "first-timer" band would partly measure the result. Each bot laner's mastery enters in coarse bands (under 50k, 50–200k, over 200k) that a few later games rarely change, and account level flags fresh accounts.

Elo changes matchups

A hook is a different threat in Emerald than in Master. Diamond+ and Emerald share a common counter term plus an elo deviation whose size is estimated, so Emerald fills sparse Diamond+ cells only as much as the two actually agree.

Small samples and the winner's curse

Sort 1,000 matchup cells by raw win rate and the top is mostly 40-game flukes. Every block's prior width is fitted by empirical Bayes, so rare cells shrink toward zero and every row carries a 90% interval.

Who gets sampled

Players sampled because they are Diamond today got there by winning. In their older, lower-elo games their team won about 57% of the time, against about 50% in their Diamond+ games. The model controls for how many sampled players sit on each team, separately for each elo bracket, so their streaks don't leak into champion numbers. Each game's elo comes from the average of all ten ranks, not the sampled player's.

No peeking at the result

Conditioning on game length, gold or KDA hides the very effect being measured, and post-game rank leaks the result through LP. Only pre-game facts are controls. Remakes, AFK and griefing surrenders are removed before fitting.

What it can't see

  • Pick order is not in Riot's match data, so blind picks and counterpicks can't be separated. The mastery and role controls absorb much of who chooses to counterpick, but not all of it.
  • Duo-queue status isn't exposed either. Practiced duos inflate some synergy pairs a little.
  • Champion mastery and rank are read after the game, so they enter only as coarse bands, never as the outcome-adjacent detail.
  • A counter here is an average over everyone who played it. Your own comfort on a champion can easily be worth more than the matchup.
